Hawks and Doves

Birds of one species meet over food. A hawk always fights. A dove only displays, and backs down if attacked. Whichever strategy earns more food per contest leaves more offspring. Which strategy wins, and does one have to win at all?

The hawk–dove game comes from John Maynard Smith and George Price, who used it to explain why most animal fights are ritual displays rather than all-out battles. These are two behavior types in one species, not real hawks and doves.
Maynard Smith, J., & G. R. Price (1973). The logic of animal conflict. Nature 246: 15–18.
