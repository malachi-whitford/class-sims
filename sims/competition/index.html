Competition & Coexistence

When two species use the same limited resources, does one drive the other out, or can they live together? Change how strongly each species competes and watch the populations over time and on the phase plane.

The equations
dN₁/dt = r₁N₁ (K₁ − N₁ − α₁₂N₂) / K₁
dN₂/dt = r₂N₂ (K₂ − N₂ − α₂₁N₁) / K₂

Each species grows logistically, but individuals of the other species take up some of the "room" under its carrying capacity. The competition coefficients (α) convert the other species into equivalents of your own species.

Two seed-eating rodents

Desert rodents such as kangaroo rats and pocket mice eat seeds. Each species' curve shows how often it eats seeds of each size. Where the curves overlap, they compete.

How overlap becomes α
α₁₂ = Σ u₁u₂ / Σ u₁²

u₁ and u₂ are the heights of the two curves at each seed size. α₁₂ is the overlap divided by how much species 1 overlaps with itself. A wide-niched species overlaps less with itself, so a narrow specialist feels a wide generalist less than the generalist feels the specialist. Try giving the two species different widths.
