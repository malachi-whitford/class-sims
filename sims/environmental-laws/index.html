Environmental Laws in the Field

From the perspective of a consulting or agency biologist: what triggers each law, what you survey for, and what goes in your report.

Updated October 2026. Three federal frameworks have shifted recently: CEQ's NEPA regulations are gone (agencies now use their own procedures), the federal ESA regulatory definition of "harm" was rescinded (effective September 2026, under litigation), and a narrower WOTUS rule is proposed but not final. State laws (CEQA, CESA, Porter-Cologne, Fish & Game Code) are not affected by these federal changes.

Impact analysis & the mitigation hierarchy

The strongest biological reports show how the project avoids impacts first, then minimizes what's left, and only then compensates. Work through each tool below.

1 · The mitigation hierarchy (CEQA Guidelines §15370)

Click each level. Agencies expect you to show why a higher level wasn't feasible before relying on a lower one.

2 · Types of impacts

TypeDefinitionField examples
DirectCaused by the project at the same time and place (CEQA §15358(a)(1))Grading removes habitat; equipment crushes a burrow; tree removal destroys a nest
IndirectCaused by the project but later in time or farther away, and reasonably foreseeable (§15358(a)(2))Noise or lighting disturbs nesting birds; runoff sediments a downslope pool; new residents' pets prey on wildlife; weeds spread from disturbed soil
PermanentHabitat or individuals lost for the life of the projectBuilding pads, roads, solar arrays, fill in wetlands, mortality
TemporaryDisturbance that ends and is restored, typically within a year or twoStaging areas, temporary access roads, pipeline trenches that are backfilled and reseeded
CumulativeThe project's contribution combined with past, present, and reasonably foreseeable projects (§15355, §15130)Several solar projects together fragment a kit fox movement corridor

3 · Is the impact significant?

CEQA asks whether the project would have a "substantial adverse effect." There's no single formula; this checklist shows the factors reviewers expect you to weigh and discuss.

4 · Write an enforceable mitigation measure

Paste or write a measure. The checker looks for the parts reviewers and agencies expect: who, what, when, where, how much, a performance standard, and reporting.
