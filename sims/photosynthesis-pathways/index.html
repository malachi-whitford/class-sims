Inside the chloroplast

Rubisco can't always tell CO₂ from O₂

Rubisco attaches either CO₂ (carboxylation, useful) or O₂ (oxygenation, wasteful) to RuBP. Oxygenation makes 2‑phosphoglycolate, which the plant must recycle through photorespiration — costing ATP and releasing CO₂ that was already fixed. Molecule numbers are not to scale (real air has ~500 O₂ for every CO₂).

Three strategies for fixing carbon

C₄ and CAM plants use the enzyme PEP carboxylase (which ignores O₂) to capture CO₂ first as a 4‑carbon acid, then release it around Rubisco at high concentration. C₄ separates the steps in space (mesophyll → bundle sheath); CAM separates them in time (night → day). Both cost extra ATP. Set the environment and see who comes out ahead.
