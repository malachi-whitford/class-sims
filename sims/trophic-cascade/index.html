Trophic Cascade

Three identical field cages of grass, goldenrod, and grasshoppers. One has no spiders, one has hunting spiders, and one has spiders with their mouthparts glued shut: they can scare grasshoppers but never eat them. Comparing the three separates the effect of eating prey (consumptive effects) from the effect of scaring prey (non-consumptive effects).

Based on Oswald Schmitz's field cages in Connecticut old fields, with the nursery web spider Pisaurina mira and the red-legged grasshopper Melanoplus femurrubrum. Grasshoppers prefer grass, but when spiders are around they switch to goldenrod (Solidago rugosa), which gives them cover. Schmitz glued spiders' mouthparts to test whether fear alone could cause a trophic cascade. Rates here are illustrative.
Schmitz, O. J., A. P. Beckerman, & K. M. O'Brien (1997). Behaviorally mediated trophic cascades: effects of predation risk on food web interactions. Ecology 78: 1388–1399.
