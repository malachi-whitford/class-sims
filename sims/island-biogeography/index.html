Island Biogeography

Why do big islands near the mainland have more species than small, remote ones? MacArthur and Wilson (1967) proposed that the number of species on an island is a balance between new species arriving and resident species going extinct. The same ideas apply to "habitat islands" like mountaintops, lakes, caves, and fragments of habitat surrounded by farms or cities.

The model
Immigration I(S) = I₀ (1 − S/P)
Extinction E(S) = E₀ (S/P)
Equilibrium Ŝ = P · I₀ / (I₀ + E₀)

I₀ (the rate when the island is empty) shrinks with distance: fewer seeds, spiders, and birds make it across. E₀ shrinks with area: big islands hold bigger populations and more habitats, so species are less likely to blink out.
